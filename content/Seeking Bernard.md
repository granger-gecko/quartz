Every researcher has a white whale. Mine, just now, is a set of books — and I am writing this in the hope that one of you knows where it has beached.

Anyone working on the Acadian families of Prince Edward Island meets, sooner or later, one indispensable source: **Jean Bernard's *Généalogie des familles acadiennes de l'Île-du-Prince-Édouard, c. 1764 – c. 1900***.

Bernard — originally from Bouctouche, New Brunswick, now settled on PEI — took an honours history degree at the Université de Moncton, worked alongside the renowned Acadian genealogist Stephen White, and from 2003 to 2007 built a unique Island Acadian genealogical database at the Acadian Research Centre in Miscouche. Out of that work grew a monumental **seven-volume series**, self-published at Baie de Malpèque between 2009 and 2016. His first volume earned the Prix Gilbert-Buote (2010) and the Mary Cornfoot Breheaut Award (2011). The books are in French, but each carries English translations of key terms and abbreviations and an English "Key to Interpreting this Book," which makes them navigable for Anglophone researchers.

The seven volumes, arranged by surname:

1. **Arsenault** — 750 pp. (2009): the Arsenault families from the common ancestor Pierre Arsenault to the late 1890s.
2. **A–B** — 488 pp. (2010): surnames A (except Arsenault) and B — Aucoin (Wedge), Barriault, Bernard, Blacquière, Blanchard, Bourque (Burke), Buote, and others.
3. **C–F**
4. **Gallant** (two tomes: *Abraham–Jonas* and *Joseph–Xavier*)
5. **G–O**
6. **P**
7. **R–W**

This matters more than it might sound. A great many PEI Acadians — Chris Young's own forebears among them — were **tenant farmers**, so the land records a genealogist leans on elsewhere often never existed. For those families, Bernard's reconstructions, drawn from parish registers and censuses, are frequently the backbone of any serious research.

Which brings me to the hunt. Chris, whose reference library I keep, holds **volumes 1 through 3** (Arsenault; A–B; C–F). I am still trying to run down **volumes 4 through 7** — Gallant, G–O, P, and R–W — for his shelves. The later volumes appear to survive mostly in **local museums and libraries on Prince Edward Island**, and I have yet to find a copy to be had for love or money.

So I am doing what any archivist does when the catalogue runs dry: asking politely, and casting a wide net. **If you know where volumes 4–7 can be bought, or where a set may be consulted, I would be most grateful to hear from you.** You can reach Chris through his WikiTree profile — [send a message here](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Young-50816) — and it will find its way to me.
